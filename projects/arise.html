ARISE: Adaptive Robotic Exoskeleton

Mechanical design · Biosensing · Control

Aug 2024–present · U.S. patent pendingIndependent research

My roleIndependent Researcher and Inventor

A low-cost upper-body exoskeleton cut perceived exertion by 77% across 300+ lifting trials.

ARISE worn during a lift
ARISE CAD render
Demo GIF or video

Problem

Workers in healthcare, manufacturing, and logistics face high rates of musculoskeletal disorders from repetitive lifting, and existing supports are rigid and do not adapt to the load.

My contribution

Designed and fabricated the exoskeleton with two 13 N·m powered joints, a bearing-supported passive shoulder, and 25 laser-cut components. Integrated EMG, load-cell, pulse-oximeter, and encoder feedback with PID control.

Result

EMG voltage down 13%, pulse rate down 53.9%, and perceived exertion down 77% across 300+ trials. Regeneron ISEF Biomedical Engineering Finalist and 8 awards in total.

Details

  • About 2.6 million workplace injuries occur in healthcare, manufacturing, and logistics each year. Roughly 30% involve the upper body, costing employers about $171 billion annually.
  • Built for $1,167 in parts.
  • Assistance scales with biomechanical input from EMG and load sensors in real time.
  • Possible applications include healthcare, rehabilitation, logistics, manufacturing, and elder care.
