How the header works

The header is a 2D transient heat-conduction model on a jittered triangular mesh: ∂T/∂t = α∇²T − h(T − T∞). Each frame takes an explicit finite-difference step, using each node’s mesh neighbors for the Laplacian. From this, heat spreads outward while the −h term (convection to ambient) cools it. The cursor acts as a heated probe that holds the nodes beneath it at a Gaussian profile, Tprobe = Tmax·exp(−|x − xc|²/2σ²), and a click launches an expanding ring of heat. The glow around the photo acts as a steady source. Colors map ΔT from 0 to 40 °C, and the isotherms at 10, 20, and 30 °C are traced every frame by marching triangles.

Problem

Workers in healthcare, manufacturing, and logistics face high rates of musculoskeletal disorders from repetitive lifting (~2.6M injured yearly), and existing supports are rigid and do not adapt to the load.

My contribution

Designed and fabricated the exoskeleton with two 13 N·m powered joints, a bearing-supported passive shoulder, and 25 laser-cut components. Integrated EMG, load-cell, pulse-oximeter, and encoder feedback with PIDF control.

Result

EMG voltage decreased 13%, pulse rate decreased 53.9%, perceived exertion decreased 77%, and time decreased 84.7% across 300+ trials. Potential in 20+ fields such as emergency response, rehabilitation, etc.

Problem

My contribution

Trained a YOLOv8 detector on 3,000+ labeled frames from the ROV camera. Developed a geometry-based estimator that computes depth, distance, and bearing from the bounding box and known camera parameters, and integrated both into the ROS control system.

Custom Wheels for Increased Traction on Martian Terrain

Independent Researcher and InventorOct 2022 – Apr 2023

3D-printed rover wheel with geometry-optimized V-beams and filtering protrusions, traction-tested in MMS-1 Mars simulant.

  • Wheel & Tread Design
  • Fusion360 (CAD)
  • FDM 3D Printing
  • Traction Testing
  • Iterative Prototyping
  • Experimental Design
  • Regolith Simulant Testing
  • Geometric Optimization

PaperSlides

Problem

Mars rovers lose mobility to fine regolith and uneven ground. Wheels on Curiosity and Perseverance have suffered punctures, slippage, and sand buildup.

My contribution

Designed, 3D-printed in PLA+, and traction-tested three wheel prototypes in MMS-1 Mars simulant, refining the tread geometry after each round. The final 4" wheel pairs V-shaped multidirectional tread beams with sand-filtering protrusions to grip loose, uneven ground.

Result

The third prototype delivered the best traction of the three: its filtering boxes let regolith pass through instead of building up, and the V-beams added side-to-side grip, all while keeping the wheel light.

VIGIL: Drone-Guided Smart Glasses

Product Designer and Co-InventorSep 2026

Drone-guided smart glasses that help blind and low-vision users find objects through spoken and tactile cues. 3rd Place Overall Winner (of 268 Teams) at HackGT 13.

  • Wearable Hardware Design
  • Fusion 360 (CAD)
  • 3D Printing
  • ESP32-CAM
  • Arduino Nano ESP32
  • Time-of-Flight Sensors
  • SG90 Servos
  • Haptic Feedback
  • Power & Signal Routing
Project details →

Demo VideoDevpostSlides

Problem

Finding an everyday object in an unfamiliar or cluttered room is hard for blind and low-vision people, who often have to search by hand or ask someone for help. The goal was a hands-free system where the user simply asks for an object and is guided to it.

My contribution

Designed and 3D-printed custom mounts that retrofit off-the-shelf sunglasses with an ESP32-CAM, an Arduino Nano ESP32, two VL53L0X time-of-flight sensors, and two SG90 servos. Routed power and signals for reliable haptic feedback on a moving user.

Result

A working prototype in 36 hours: the user asks for an object through AirPods, a DJI Tello searches the room with a scan, score, hop strategy, and the glasses tap left or right to guide the user. The drone’s steering controller, built from a 1,446-neuron fruit-fly connectome, held 8.7° pointing error versus 29.5° with the circuit frozen.

TEND: Cartesian Gripper with Tactile & Vision Assistance

Lead Mechanical & Hardware Designer, Co-InventorNov 2026

A 7-degree-of-freedom (DoF) robotic hand with tactile sensing and vision for dexterous tasks, built to help people with disabilities or limited grip reclaim the everyday tasks most take for granted.

  • Fusion 360 (CAD)
  • Herringbone Rack-and-Pinion
  • 3D Printing
  • Digital Twin Simulation
  • Press-Fit Tolerancing
  • ESP32-C6
  • Tactile Sensing
  • Current Sensing
  • Python
  • Gemini API
Project details →

Demo VideoDevpostSlides

Problem

More than 50 million US adults have arthritis, and 92% of people with rheumatic conditions struggle to open medicine packaging. Assistive robotic arms that could help cost around $35,000.

My contribution

Developed a Cartesian Hand mechanism in Fusion 360, inspired by Duke’s General Robotics Lab, and modified it so all four fingers slide independently, adding 4 DoF that let the hand twist grasped objects. Redesigned the joints around 180° servos for about 25 mm of travel per finger, and designed a 16-tooth herringbone rack-and-pinion for each joint, tuning the press fit with a test coupon. Iterated 17 printed parts until every finger slid smoothly.

Result

A sensor-integrated prototype in 24 hours with seven actuated motions, current-controlled grip, and a tactile array streaming at about 18 frames per second. In simulation, the twist routine ran at about 6 s per cycle and removed the cap in under 45 s.

Compact Bevel Differential Gearbox

Mechanism Designer2025

A 1:1 bevel-gear differential gearbox that combines two servos to drive pitch and roll in one compact housing, giving each motion the torque of both servos.

  • Bevel Gear Design
  • Differential Mechanisms
  • Java
  • Fusion 360 (CAD)
  • 3D Printing
  • PWM Control
  • Axon MAX Servos
  • FR4 Fabrication
  • Weight Optimization
Project details →

Problem

Driving pitch and roll independently usually takes a separate servo and gear stage for each axis, which is complex and bulky at the end of an arm.

My contribution

Designed a symmetric bevel-gear differential driven by two Axon MAX servos: turning both servos the same way pitches the output, and turning them in opposite directions rolls it. Supported by FR4 side walls, a 3D-printed housing, custom-printed bevel gears, and 32 mm flanged bearings.

Result

Both servos share the load on every motion, so each axis gets the torque of two servos from one compact housing. It accurately reached every position across its full pitch and roll range.

Cascading Telescoping Extension

Mechanism Designer2024

A multi-stage telescoping boxtube extension, driven by a threaded rod and cascade string rigging, that reaches full extension in about one second.

  • Cascade String Rigging
  • Threaded-Rod Drive
  • Machining
  • Aluminum Extrusion
  • Carbon-Fiber Nylon Printing
  • Tolerancing
  • Modular Design
  • Fusion 360 (CAD)
Project details →

Problem

Telescoping extensions are complex to build and hard to maintain, and most are driven entirely by string and pulleys, which can skip or lose tension under load. A threaded rod cannot skip.

My contribution

Designed a multi-stage extension with a threaded-rod first stage and a string-driven cascade on every stage after it, so all stages move together. Built it from aluminum extrusion with nylon and carbon-fiber prints at high-stress points, low-friction linear bearings between stages, and ±0.25 mm modeled clearances. Located every hole with 3D-printed drill templates for fast, repeatable fabrication without machining.

Result

Driven by a 3.7:1 goBILDA motor, it reaches full extension in about one second and carries up to 10 lb at full extension. Each stage is modular and swaps out with 8 screws.
