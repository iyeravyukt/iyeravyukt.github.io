How the header works

The header is a 2D transient heat-conduction model on a jittered triangular mesh: ∂T/∂t = α∇²T − h(T − T∞). Each frame takes an explicit finite-difference step, using each node’s mesh neighbors for the Laplacian. From this, heat spreads outward while the −h term (convection to ambient) cools it. The cursor acts as a heated probe that holds the nodes beneath it at a Gaussian profile, Tprobe = Tmax·exp(−|x − xc|²/2σ²), and a click launches an expanding ring of heat. The glow around the photo acts as a steady source. Colors map ΔT from 0 to 40 °C, and the isotherms at 10, 20, and 30 °C are traced every frame by marching triangles.

ARISE: Adaptive Robotic Exoskeleton

Independent Researcher and InventorAug 2024 – Present

A low-cost upper-body exoskeleton cut exertion by 53.7% across 300+ lifting trials.

ISEF pagePaperPosterSlidesFull project page →

Problem

Workers in healthcare, manufacturing, and logistics face high rates of musculoskeletal disorders from repetitive lifting (~2.6M injured yearly), and existing supports are rigid and do not adapt to the load.

My contribution

Designed and fabricated the exoskeleton with two 13 N·m powered joints, a bearing-supported passive shoulder, and 25 laser-cut components. Integrated EMG, load-cell, pulse-oximeter, and encoder feedback with PIDF control.

Result

EMG voltage decreased 13%, pulse rate decreased 53.9%, perceived exertion decreased 77%, and time decreased 84.7% across 300+ trials. Potential in 20+ fields such as emergency response, rehabilitation, etc.

ROV Detection and Pose EstimationMIT Beaver Works Summer Institute

Autonomous Underwater Vehicle Student ResearcherJun 2025 – Aug 2025

A YOLOv8 detector and geometric pose estimator ran in real time on a Raspberry Pi with 95% accuracy.

Problem

The final challenge was autonomous light tag between underwater robots, which needs reliable detection and relative pose in changing lighting, turbidity, and occlusion.

My contribution

Trained a YOLOv8 detector on 3,000+ labeled frames from the ROV camera. Developed a geometry-based estimator that computes depth, distance, and bearing from the bounding box and known camera parameters, and integrated both into the ROS control system.

Result

95% classification accuracy with real-time inference on a Raspberry Pi, and real-time relative pose during pool tests. Selected for the program at under 9% acceptance.

Custom Wheels for Increased Traction on Martian Terrain

Independent Researcher and Inventor

Three 3D-printed rover wheel designs, traction-tested in MMS-1 Mars simulant.

PaperSlides

Problem

Mars rovers lose mobility to fine regolith and uneven ground. Wheels on Curiosity and Perseverance have suffered punctures, slippage, and sand buildup.

My contribution

Designed, 3D-printed in PLA+, and tested three wheel prototypes in MMS-1 Mars simulant, iterating after each round.

Result

The third prototype achieved the best traction, with sand-filtering boxes and V-shaped multidirectional tread beams, while balancing weight.

  • Pittsburgh Regional Science & Engineering Fair – Outstanding Presentation Award
  • PJAS – 2nd Place, Regional

Compact Bevel Differential Gearbox

2024–25 seasonFTC Team 8393

My roleMechanism design

A 1:1 differential that controls pitch and roll from two servos inside a very tight footprint.

Project details →

Problem

The scoring system needed two synchronized rotational axes in a space too small for a conventional spur-gear transmission.

My contribution

Designed a symmetric bevel-gear differential that splits torque between two Axon MAX servos, built from FR4 walls, a 3D-printed housing, custom printed bevel gears, and 32 mm flanged bearings.

Result

Cut housing mass through CAD iteration while holding torque under load. Ran on the 2024–25 V3 competition robot.

Cascading Telescoping Extension

FTC subsystemFTC Team 8393

My roleMechanism design

A multi-stage extension that reaches full height in about one second and carries 10 lb at the end.

Project details →

Problem

The robot needed rapid vertical reach that stayed rigid under load and was easy to service.

My contribution

Designed a threaded-rod first stage with a string-driven cascade on every stage after it. Used aluminum extrusion with nylon and carbon-fiber prints at high-stress points, modeled to ±0.25 mm clearances.

Result

Driven by a 3.7:1 goBILDA motor, it reaches full extension in about one second, carries up to 10 lb, and each stage swaps out with 4 screws.
